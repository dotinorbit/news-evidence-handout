# Titanic reports and evidence

A two-page critical-reading exercise for secondary English. Learners compare six short excerpts from two 1912 U.S. newspapers and practice distinguishing published claims from established facts, qualifying numerical claims, and recognizing what the evidence cannot tell them.

## Get the handout

- [Printable PDF](titanic-reports-and-evidence.pdf): fixed two-page layout
- [Editable DOCX](titanic-reports-and-evidence.docx): adapt the wording or layout in a compatible word processor

**Print or share page 1 only with learners.** Page 2 contains teacher guidance and a 10-point rubric. Students answer the four questions on separate paper. No prior Titanic knowledge or source search is required. Discuss the loss of life sensitively.

## Sources and provenance

The excerpts come from:
- *The Evening World*, New York, April 15, 1912, Final Edition, page 1: [Library of Virginia scan](https://www.lva.virginia.gov/events/exhibitions/titanic/img/newspaper_coverage/newspaper_9.jpg)
- *The Times Dispatch*, Richmond, Virginia, April 16, 1912, page 1: [Library of Virginia scan](https://www.lva.virginia.gov/events/exhibitions/titanic/img/newspaper_coverage/newspaper_8.jpg)

See the [Library of Virginia exhibition archive](https://www.lva.virginia.gov/events/exhibitions/titanic/newspaper_coverage.php) for context. All six excerpts were visually checked against these scans on October 2, 2026. Excerpt labels are added; capitalization and line breaks are normalized, and line-break hyphenation is rejoined. The excerpts are separate selections, not complete articles.

The teacher page includes additional Library of Congress references. Those links may be unavailable in some environments; the Library of Virginia scans above are the verification sources.
